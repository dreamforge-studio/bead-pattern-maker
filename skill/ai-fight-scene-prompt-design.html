What job it does, what it saves

AI fight scenes look staged for one reason: the prompt has no causality and no feedback. Hits land without reaction, fighters teleport, swords swing at air. This method turns 'impact' into engineering — phase table first to lock the rhythm skeleton, full prompts only after you approve it, QA pass before anything is generated — and removes the regenerate-and-pray cycle that eats most of an action scene's budget.

What's inside / the method

  • Four iron rules: split the action into timed segments; build an action cause-and-effect chain; write environmental feedback into every exchange; lock your assets
  • Pacing doctrine: fast but never chaotic; slow motion reserved for the one decisive hit
  • Coverage-angle matrix that picks camera positions from the story beats
  • Impact-frame and flash-frame techniques
  • Three-angle diagnosis for footage that comes back wrong
  • Covers two-character duels, close-quarters combat, vehicle chases, group battles, giant-creature and mecha set pieces, and single-take fights
  • Included case: a complete 15-second breakdown (teaching example) — a swordswoman against a giant monster, including deliberately designed 'failed hits' like a heavy strike that gets deflected
  • Phase-table template and pre-generation QA checklist included. Prompt design only — generation runs in your own video pipeline
